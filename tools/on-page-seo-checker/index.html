What the checker looks at

On-page SEO is everything a search engine reads on your page before it decides where you rank. Most of it lives in the HTML — and most of it is fixable in an afternoon. The checker scores the checks below; the fix list tells you what to change first.

The checks

  • Title tag — present, unique, right length (under ~60 characters), with your keyword near the front
  • Meta description — present and under ~155 characters (not a ranking factor, but the #1 click-through lever)
  • H1 — exactly one, containing your core keyword (search engines read it as the page's theme)
  • Heading hierarchy — H1 → H2 → H3 in order, no skipped levels
  • Image alt text — every image described; missing alt = lost Google Images traffic
  • Canonical — self-referencing canonical prevents duplicate-content dilution
  • Structured data — JSON-LD schema helps search engines understand and display your page
  • Internal links — links to your own pages pass authority and guide crawlers
  • Language, viewport, robots — the technical basics that gate everything else

Why on-page alone isn't enough

On-page SEO tells Google what your page is about. Off-page SEO — the backlinks pointing at it — tells Google whether anyone believes you. A perfect page with no backlinks rarely outranks a good page with authority. That's why our service system pairs clean on-page work with editorial backlinks from American publishers: content tells the story, links make it credible.
