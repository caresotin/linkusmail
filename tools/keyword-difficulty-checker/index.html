How the estimate works

Keyword difficulty (KD) tools score how hard it is to rank in the top 10 for a keyword. Most paid tools hide their math. This one doesn't — it asks you to look at the actual search results and scores what you see, the way an SEO would.

The six signals

  • Homepages vs inner pages — if the top 10 are mostly homepages of big sites, competition is brutal; mostly inner pages means there's room
  • Authority of the top 10 — household names (Wikipedia, Amazon, major media) are hard to outrank; niche sites you can beat
  • Direct answers — if Google already answers the query at the top (calculators, snippets), people may never click through
  • Ads & shopping — heavy ads mean commercial intent and big spenders; you'll need real authority to compete
  • Content format — listicles, videos, tools or products: you must match the format users expect
  • Freshness — year-stamped queries ("2026") and trending topics have less entrenched competition

What the score means

0–29: genuinely winnable with solid content and a handful of good backlinks. 30–49: winnable with better-than-average content and a real link-building effort. 50–69: expect a long campaign — this is where editorial backlinks start to matter a lot. 70–100: dominated by giants; consider a longer-tail variation of the keyword instead.
